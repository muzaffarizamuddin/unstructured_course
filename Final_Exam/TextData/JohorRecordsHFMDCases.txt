Johor records 6,987 HFMD cases.

ISKANDAR PUTERI: A total 6,987 cases of hand, foot and mouth disease (HFMD) have been reported up to yesterday, the Johor State Assembly was told today.
This was a drastic increase compared with 104 cases during the same period last year.
State Health and Unity Committee chairman Ling Tian Soon said most of the cases occurred among children aged six and below (6,048 cases or 86.6 per cent).
He said this was followed by children aged seven to 12, with 816 cases (11.7 per cent).
The rest of the cases were those aged 13 and above.
He said five active HFMD outbreaks were reported in Kota Tinggi district (two), Johor Baru (two) and Batu Pahat (one), compared with 28 active HFMD outbreaks in the previous epidemiological week.
"As of yesterday, there were 100 HFMD outbreaks in Johor involving nurseries (42), private homes (22), tabika (13), kindergartens (nine), pre-school (seven), primary schools (three), day-care centres (two) and others (two).
"However, the situation is still under control. This is proven by the significant drop in HFMD cases reported in the state," he said in his reply to an oral question.
On monkeypox, Ling said no case had been reported in Malaysia to date.
"The Johor Health Department is in a state of readiness," he said to a question from Datuk Ramlee Bohani (BN-Kempas) on the efforts taken by the state government to curb the spread of infectious diseases and whether the state health facilities were prepared if the diseases spread drastically. — BERNAMA
