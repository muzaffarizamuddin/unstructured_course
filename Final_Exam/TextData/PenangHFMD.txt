GEORGE TOWN: Penang recorded 4,263 cases of Hand, Foot and Mouth Disease (HFMD) between the 1st and 23rd epidemiological week (EW) — a 32-fold increase compared to last year.

In 2021, during the Covid-19 pandemic, there were 176 cases during the same period.

State Health Committee chairman Dr Norlela Ariffin said the southwest district recorded the highest number of cases at 1,069 this year compared to 50 cases last year followed by Seberang Prai Tengah (896 cases compared to 54 cases) and Seberang Prai Utara (880 cases compared to 20 cases).

She said the northeast district had 851 cases this year compared to 46 cases last year while Seberang Prai Selatan (567 cases compared to eight).

"Penang is now the fourth highest in terms of HFMD clusters in the country at 118," she said today.


There are two viruses which can result in death, namely EV71 and Coxsackie.

There are three children currently at intensive care units (ICU), but none of them are from Penang.

Back in 2018, Penang recorded one HFMD death.

Norlela said, as of last Saturday (June 11), 120 premises with HFMD cases had been ordered to close.

"To date, 118 premises have been allowed to reopen," she added.

Of the total 120 premises, 49 were taska; tadika (38), pre-schools (17), tabika (10), care centres (three) schools (two) and others (one).

Norlela said the Health Ministry and the state Health department had ordered any premises with two HFMD cases to be closed for six days.

Under the law, they are required to close for up to 10 days.

"The state Health Department reduced the number of days as the virus incubation period is between three and five days. The virus can live between seven and 10 days," she said, adding that the shorter closure order would prevent parents from sending their possibly-infected children to other taska/tadika.
