HFMD cases drop further with only two cases remaining in ICUs

By Teoh Pei Ying - June 14, 2022 @ 11:44am

KUALA LUMPUR: Hand, foot mouth disease (HFMD) cases dropped 22.9 per cent during epidemiological week (EW) 23, between June 5 and 11, with only two cases under observation at intensive care units (ICU).

Health director-general Tan Sri Dr Noor Hisham Abdullah said 13,080 HFMD cases were reported in EW 23 compared to 16,954 cases the previous week.

He added that HFMD cases were on a downward trend after it peaked in EW 21.

Up to June 11, he said, the country registered 95,924 HFMD infections, a 36-fold increase compared to 2,654 cases reported in 2021 during the same period of time.

"It's 3.1 times more than the 30,880 cases recorded in 2019 before the pandemic hit," he said in a statement today.


Dr Noor Hisham said three HFMD patients were admitted to the ICU this year.

He said that first and second cases were still under close observation due to encephalitis complications after being infected with enterovirus 71 (EV71), one of the predominant viruses spreading in the community.

He added that the third patient had recovered and was discharged.

The other two predominant viruses are the Coxsackie A16 (CA16) and Coxsackie A6 (CA6).

Dr Noor Hisham said 90 per cent of the cases involved children aged six-years-old and below, eight per-cent involved patients aged seven to 12, while two per cent were those aged 12 and above.

As of June 11, Selangor recorded the highest HFMD cases at 26,799, followed by Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya with 10,739 cases, while Perak had 9,744 cases.

Johor recorded 6,326 cases to date, Kelantan with 6,153 infections, and Melaka (5,540 cases) while numbers in other states and federal territories were below 5,500.

The health director-general said 1,882 outbreaks were recorded nationwide with the highest reported in Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya with 602 recorded, followed by Sarawak (265 outbreaks) and Selangor (233 outbreaks).

Most of these outbreaks, he said occurred in nurseries, kindergartens and pre-schools with 1,102 outbreaks, private residential areas (696 outbreaks) and day care centres (55 outbreaks).

The Health Ministry has taken control and preventive measures nationwide to curb the spread of HFMD in the country.

These include 55,227 checks conducted at premises such as nurseries, kindergartens and preschools, where 1,125 premises were ordered to cease operations under the Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases Act 1988; 297 townhall meetings, and 167,171 health education sessions.

Dr Noor Hisham warned that though HFMD cases had been on the downward trend for the past two weeks, the people must not let their guard down.

He reminded parents and guardians not to bring their children to public areas if the young ones displayed HFMD symptoms.

He again called on nurseries, kindergartens and pre-school operators to conduct screening before a child was allowed into the premises and the same reminder was issued to indoor playground operators, as well as to clean and disinfect their facilities at least twice a day.
