Prolonged global supply shortcoming to meet surging demand

KUALA LUMPUR: As major economies reopen at a fast pace from around early 2021, industry reports point towards a prolonged difficulty faced by global producers to meet the rapidly rising demand, Bank Negara Malaysia said.

The central bank, in its Annual Report 2021 released today, said globally, the Covid-19 pandemic had hampered the production and distribution of goods as containment measures were imposed.

"For example, freight costs became elevated partly due to container shortages. This led to higher import prices, eventually affecting some domestic producers.

"As these cost pressures persisted, some firms have passed on the higher costs to their customers," it said.

Bank Negara said in addition, energy prices (crude oil and natural gas) had become more expensive worldwide.

"While the direct impact to Malaysia was contained due to price ceilings on RON95 and diesel, there were indirect effects, for instance through costlier fertiliser (a by-product of natural gas).

"Furthermore, global prices of food grains such as soybean and corn reached multi-year high in 2021," it said.

Bank Negara said for countries like Malaysia that relied on imports of these items as animal feed, this had resulted in increased costs for livestock operators, which filtered into higher chicken and egg prices for consumers.

"Domestically, some food producers also faced difficulties to immediately meet the increased consumer demand as restrictions were lifted.

"Adverse weather conditions compounded these difficulties, for instance, by causing price increases for fresh vegetables at end-2021.

"Taking together, all these factors combined are pushing prices higher," it added.
