FMD: Sarawak on high alert.

KUALA LUMPUR: Kuching, Serian, Sri Aman, Miri and Limbang in Sarawak have been declared as Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) Control Area.
Sarawak Agriculture Modernisation and Regional Development Minister Datuk Seri Dr Stephen Rundi Utom said the areas or divisions were bordering West Kalimantan and North Kalimantan where active outbreaks of FMD were occurring among the cattle, buffalo, goat and sheep population.
"This declaration is made to allow the Department of Veterinary Services Sarawak to impose and take all the necessary control measures to prevent the introduction of FMD into Sarawak," he said.
Rundi said control measures were in place including import ban on livestock and related products from Indonesia, surveillance of livestock in high risk areas and veterinary inspections at the entry point into the state. 
"FMD is an acute and highly contagious viral infection on both domestic and wild cloven-hooved animals which include cattle, buffalo, domesticated deer, goat, sheep, pigs as well as wild boar and deer."
Rundi said the disease spreads from animal to animal through nasal discharges, contaminated feeds, beddings and other fomites.
"However, FMD is not a zoonotic disease and is not a public health concern as distinct from the Hand, Foot and Mouth Disease affecting our young children caused by a different virus," he said.
Rundi also said Sarawak has been recognised as an FMD free zone without vaccination by the World Organisation for Animal Health since May 2004.
